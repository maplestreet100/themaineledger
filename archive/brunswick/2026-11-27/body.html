Data centers · Top story

Brunswick extends its data center pause to late May

The Council has added about six more months, to late May, to Brunswick's pause on new or expanded data centers that use 1 megawatt of power or more. The town wants the time to study the power grid. Brunswick already has one data center, run by FirstLight at 14​ Resilient Circle.

Brunswick is not alone. Scarborough has banned data centers, and voters in Peru, Wells and Wiscasset approved pauses. Sen. Nicole Grohoski, D-Ellsworth, has refiled a bill to pause the largest ones statewide.

Source: Town of Brunswick; Maine Department of Environmental Protection; Maine Morning Star; WMTW; Maine Senate Democrats

Real estate

Around Brunswick, more sellers are cutting prices

Across Cumberland County, about 1 in 4 homes for sale has had a price cut this fall, and buyers have more homes to pick from than a year ago. Brunswick is holding up better than its neighbors, with fewer cuts and prices that are still steady.

Source: Realtor.​com and Zillow

Roads

Brunswick is lining up a new public road into the Landing

Brunswick is lining up a new public road into Brunswick Landing from Bath Road, next to the Irving station. On Oct. 19, the Town Council accepted a state grant to help pay for it, with the town, the Landing's redevelopment authority and a private landowner sharing the rest. The work would straighten the Allagash Drive S-curve, add a walking and biking path, and make it safer to get between Merrymeeting Plaza and the Landing. The project still needs a final council vote after the Navy finishes testing the soil there.

Source: Town of Brunswick; Brunswick Landing (MRRA); Map: OpenStreetMap

Weather

November feels more like early fall

Monday hit 67 degrees in Portland, 21 above normal and three short of the date's record. Casco Bay read 50 degrees Tuesday afternoon, the warmest for the date since at least 2016. No snow has fallen yet, and rain is more than an inch below normal. Cumberland County is in a severe drought, and parts of it have reached extreme drought, one step worse. Since 2000, extreme drought this late in the year has only happened last year and this year.

Source: National Weather Service, Gray; NOAA; U.S. Drought Monitor

Power bill

CMP bills rise about $5 a month in January

A typical CMP household will pay about $5 more a month for power starting Jan. 1, the state says. The state sets the price once a year through bidding, and New England power prices largely track natural gas. Brunswick, Topsham and Bath are all CMP towns and pay the same price. Even after the increase, it remains well below the 2023 price. That 2023 spike came when global instability and war sent natural gas prices soaring.

Source: Maine Public Utilities Commission; Central Maine Power; Versant Power

Apartments

Hundreds of new apartments are on the way in Brunswick

Brunswick has approved about 770 new homes in roughly four years, most of them in apartment buildings and many at Brunswick Landing, the former Navy base. Some of the larger projects there, like Atlantic Pointe, are finished. Most of the approvals came in the first year. As of March, nearly 300 more homes, including condos and house lots, were still under review. The town's list was last updated in March, so some projects may have moved ahead since.

Downtown

Four-story condo building planned for former J&J Cleaners site

Holman Homes plans The Jay, a four-story building with 34 condos over first-floor commercial space and an underground garage, at 53 and 69 Maine St., the former J&J Cleaners and the old Embark Restaurant building. The site was a gas station and a dry cleaner; the developer puts the cleanup at about $1.3 million. On Oct. 19 the Town Council voted 7-2 for a contract zone cutting required parking from 87 spaces to 41. Holman Homes says it expects to finish in fall 2028.

Source: Town of Brunswick; Press Herald; Holman Homes

Wildlife

The winter sea ducks are back

Brunswick's winter birds are arriving on the coast. On Nov. 15, observers logged surf scoters and a red-throated loon in Brunswick, and earlier in the month black ducks and red-breasted mergansers. Buffleheads, small black-and-white divers, turned up in Harpswell and West Bath on Nov. 13. Many will winter here, feeding in the cold, open water of Casco Bay and the coves.

Source: iNaturalist observations; Maine Audubon; Illustration: The Maine Ledger
