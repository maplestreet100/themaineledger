Data centers · Top story

Staff recommend extending the data-center pause; Council could vote Monday

Town staff recommend extending the pause on data centers of 1 megawatt or more by 180 days, and the Council could vote Monday, Nov. 16, if it suspends its rules after the public hearing. The pause, adopted in June, otherwise ends Nov. 28. A Nov. 9 memo says the extra time would go to finding a consultant to study grid impacts and weighing the legal steps for a possible ban. The meeting starts at 6:30 p.m. and is open to the public.

Statewide

Where Brunswick's data-center pause fits in Maine

Brunswick's pause covers data centers of 1 megawatt or more; the statewide pause Gov. Janet Mills vetoed in April would have covered 20 megawatts or more. Since then Scarborough has banned data centers, and on Election Day North Berwick voters approved a permanent ban, the Press Herald and BDN reported, while Peru, Wiscasset and Wells approved temporary pauses. Gorham's council voted to extend its pause, NEWS CENTER Maine reported Nov. 10, with final action after a Planning Board review. Towns cite power and water demand and noise; some lawmakers who backed the veto said opposition rests on incomplete or inaccurate information.

Roads

Public Works reviews the town's road and building plan

On Nov. 9, Public Works presented the town's road and building plan to the Council. The town maintains about 147 miles of road and typically paves about 5.3 miles a year, compared with a goal of about 7.4. No votes were taken; the Council holds a capital plan workshop Nov. 23, and the plan is adopted with the budget next spring.

Recreation

New basketball courts open at the MARC on Nov. 18

Parks and Recreation will unveil two new basketball courts and sports lighting at the Midcoast Athletic and Recreation Complex, 220​ Neptune Drive, with a ribbon cutting Wednesday, Nov. 18, at 4:45 p.m. The lights also serve the pickleball courts and skate park, so they can be used later in the day. Housing

Six affordable apartments proposed at Woodlawn Terrace

The Brunswick Housing Authority is asking for a zoning change at Woodlawn Terrace, 30​ Water Street, to convert former office space into six affordable apartments. The property already has 141 affordable units. The request would still need a Town Council vote, and that meeting would be open to the public.

By the numbers · Housing

Brunswick house sales ran about 10% higher over the past year

State transfer-tax filings put the median Brunswick house sale at $536,000 for the 12 months through October (condos excluded), up 10.2% from the year before. Over the same months, the median was $381,000 in Bath, $438,500 in Topsham, $649,000 in Harpswell and $780,000 in Freeport. On Realtor.com, Brunswick listings in October spent a median of 48 days on the market, down from 55 a year earlier, and 13% had a price cut, down from 21%.

Parks

Town may take a vacant Cooks Corner gas station for a park

On Monday the Council considers letting the town manager accept the donated former gas station at 101​ Bath Road. Staff say the site has been vacant more than a decade, was declared a dangerous building, and sits over an aquifer, so it cannot be redeveloped commercially. The concept is a small public park that keeps the old canopy for shade, with plantings, seating and a path to 87​ Bath Road.

Zoning

Hearing set on pavement limits at municipal buildings

Also Monday, the Council holds a public hearing on a zoning change that would let the Planning Board waive or modify pavement limits on municipal property when the town engineer confirms stormwater can be managed. Staff say the police station at 85​ Pleasant Street is at its limit and cannot add the parking the department needs. The Council could vote that night.

Wildlife

Deer and turkeys at two town hunting areas test clear of PFOS

Tissue samples from white-tailed deer and wild turkeys taken at Kate Furbish Preserve and the Maquoit Bay Conservation Area off Bunganuc showed no detectable PFOS, one of the PFAS chemicals, the state wildlife department reported. The town did the testing with federal Wildlife Services and Maine Inland Fisheries and Wildlife.

This week in history

Brunswick's town name dates to a November 1738 act

Brunswick was laid out as a township in 1717, between Pejepscot Falls and Maquoit Bay. In November 1738, Massachusetts passed an act erecting a township in York County by the name of Brunswick. Because the old calendar carried the paperwork into January 1738/39, histories usually date the incorporation to 1739.
