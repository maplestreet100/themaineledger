Data centers · Top story

A data center plan is back at the old Jay mill, as towns hit pause

The owner of the closed paper mill in Jay wants to make part of it a data center. It says it would bring 125 to 150 lasting jobs. Jay needs no town vote. It is up to the state, which began its review on Nov. 16. Brunswick, Gorham, Westbrook, Scarborough and several other towns have paused or banned big data centers. Sen. Nicole Grohoski's bill would pause them statewide until Nov. 1, 2027, which could hold up Jay's plan.

Sources: Maine Morning Star, Town of Jay, Maine DEP, Town of Brunswick, NEWS CENTER Maine, WMTW

Energy

Heating oil costs $2.29 a gallon more than a year ago

Heating oil in Maine cost an average of $5.69 a gallon in the week of Nov. 16, the U.S. Energy Information Administration says. That's $2.29 more than a year ago. Propane cost $3.68 a gallon, up 53.4 cents from a year ago. About half of Maine homes, roughly 309,000 households, heat mainly with oil or kerosene, the Census Bureau says.

Source: U.S. Energy Information Administration

Fishing

Lobster boat prices have slipped to about $4.25 a pound

The price fishermen get at the dock for lobster has slipped to about $4.25 a pound and has held there, a Nov. 17 market report from Expana says. The report called that among the lowest prices in some time. Maine boats have landed more lobster through October and into November, while processors and dealers are buying more slowly. New-shell prices are at 52-week lows, and hard-shell prices are well below their five-year averages. Holiday buying and Chinese New Year demand will be the next test, the report says.

Source: Expana

Events

Nearly 2,000 people came to the first weekend of a new Gorham barn's holiday market

Nearly 2,000 people came to the first weekend of a new holiday makers market at Cherry Hill Place, a new events barn on Route 25 in Gorham. More than 40 local vendors set up, along with food trucks and a bar. The Shaw Brothers Family Foundation built the 5,917-square-foot post-and-beam barn. It leases the barn to Sebago Brewing Co. for weddings and events. The barn holds up to 350 people. The Sunday markets run through Dec. 20, with a two-day event after Thanksgiving.

Source: Portland Press Herald

Higher education

UMaine projects a $19 million budget gap, with layoffs ahead

The University of Maine expects a $19 million budget gap in fiscal 2028. At a Nov. 2 open budget session, its leaders said closing it will take both spending cuts and new money. The university has said staff layoffs and faculty cuts will be needed, though it hopes to keep them few. Undergraduates are taking fewer credit hours, about 224,000 now, down from about 259,000 in fall 2018. Energy costs the main budget about $10.5 million a year.

Source: University of Maine

Attractions

The Maine State Museum is open again after six years, free through Dec. 31

The Maine State Museum in Augusta is open again. It reopened Oct. 24 after closing in 2020, and hundreds of people came the first day. Two of its four exhibit floors are open, including a new exhibit of a humpback whale and her calf. This is the first step of a $3 million renovation, and the director hopes to open the other two floors within two years. Hours are 10 a.m. to 4 p.m., Tuesday through Sunday, and it is free for everyone through Dec. 31.

Source: NEWS CENTER Maine

Jobs

Maine's workforce is about 12,400 people smaller than a year ago

Maine's workforce shrank by 12,413 people in a year. In September, 696,710 Mainers were working or looking for work, the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics says. The number with jobs fell by about 12,000, to 673,662. The jobless rate held at 3.3%, the same as a year earlier. These are early figures, adjusted for the seasons.

Source: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics

Real estate

Maine home sales fell for a third straight month in October

Maine home sales fell for the third month in a row in October. Maine Listings counted 1,547 single-family homes sold, 6.6% fewer than a year earlier. The median price fell 5.6%, to $402,500. The state Realtors' group says buyers now have more homes to choose from and more room to bargain.

Source: Maine Association of Realtors

Drought

Maine's rivers are running far below normal as the drought deepens

Many Maine rivers are running far below normal for November. On Thursday afternoon, the Piscataquis at Dover-Foxcroft and the Sandy at Mercer were at 9% of their normal flow, National Weather Service readings show. The Kennebec at North Sidney was at 22%, and the Penobscot at West Enfield at 31%. The weekly national drought map, out Thursday, shows 91.2% of Maine in drought. That includes 55.7% in severe drought and 16.2% in extreme drought.

Source: National Weather Service

Transportation

The state ferry fleet's first hybrid joins the Vinalhaven run

The state's first hybrid ferry now serves the run between Rockland and Vinalhaven. The M/V Captain Almer Dinsmore uses diesel and electric power, and passenger service was set to start Nov. 9. MaineDOT says it is the first hybrid diesel-electric ferry designed and built in New England. The 154-foot ferry carries up to 250 people and 23 vehicles. It cost $21.9 million, and a Federal Transit Administration grant paid $16.6 million. State officials have said it should use about a quarter less fuel.

Source: Maine Department of Transportation
