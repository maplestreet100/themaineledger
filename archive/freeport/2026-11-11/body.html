Housing · Top story

A 52-apartment plan is up for hearing next Wednesday

The Project Review Board holds a public hearing next Wednesday, Nov. 18, at 6 p.m. on a plan to replace a parking lot at the corner of Stonewood Drive and Lighthouse Road with 52 apartments in four buildings, built in two phases of 27 and then 25 homes. The hearing is open to the public. Nothing has been approved.

The town describes the site as the corner of Stonewood Drive and Lighthouse Road. The pin is at Stonewood Drive. Lighthouse Road was not in OpenStreetMap on Nov. 10, 2026, so the location is approximate. Map © OpenStreetMap contributors · Protomaps. Map for The Maine Ledger

Downtown

A new downtown tax district is headed to the state

On Oct. 20, the Town Council unanimously created a downtown tax increment financing district covering about 300 acres along Route 1 and Main Street, from Mallett Drive to the Metro Breez stop near Maine Beer Company. The district is intended to fund infrastructure, sidewalks, and public spaces. It still needs state approval before it can take effect.

Source: News Center Maine

Freeport Community Services is raising $3.5 million to expand

The food pantry and thrift shop at 53‌ D‌epot S‌treet are short on room. Freeport Community Services is raising $3.5 million to expand both and has $2.8 million in so far, with groundbreaking planned for 2027. The pantry now occupies four small rooms, so only a few people can shop at a time.

Housing

An eight-lot plan off Beech Hill Road seeks final review

The Project Review Board is scheduled to give final review next Wednesday, Nov. 18, to Fox Ridge, an eight-lot open-space subdivision off Beech Hill Road. The plan uses about 22 acres of a roughly 90-acre parcel and includes a new private road about 1,000 feet long. It is not approved yet.

Waterfront

A $218,400 grant will repair town docks on the Harraseeket

Freeport was awarded $218,400 to replace deteriorating docks and floats at Cove Road Wharf, the Commercial River Floats, and the Town Dock on the Harraseeket River. Those landings serve more than 60 town shellfish license holders, plus other commercial boats.

Source: National Fisherman  ·  Illustration for The Maine Ledger

Zoning

Downtown design rules may get simpler, more staff-level review

The Planning Board held a public hearing Nov. 4 on replacing the Design Review Ordinance. The proposal would use more straightforward standards, rename building classes as Contributing or Non-Contributing, and let staff review more projects. A posted decision was not available as of Nov. 10.

This week in history

A Nov. 16, 1936 aerial showed the Desert of Maine

On Nov. 16, 1936, an aerial photograph showed tourists walking the dunes at the Desert of Maine. The Portland Press Herald published a similar view that day under the title "What You Should See in Maine." The site had been the Tuttle family farm before it opened as a tourist attraction in 1925.
