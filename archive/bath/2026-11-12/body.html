Downtown · Top story

Front and Elm streets are being rebuilt downtown

The city and contractor McGee Construction began reconstructing Front, Summer, and Elm streets on July 6, including sidewalks, drainage, lighting, and plantings. The city says Elm Street between Water and Front is one-way eastbound, and Front Street between Elm and Summer is one-way northbound. Downtown businesses remain open, the city said.

Public safety

A new fire station is taking shape on High Street

Voters approved a $13.05 million bond in November 2022 for a new fire headquarters to replace the 1958 station at 864‌ H‌igh S‌treet. The two-story, 21,800-square-foot building is under construction at the north end of the former Morse High School property. The city's latest posted monthly update, from July, said steel, roof-deck, and utility work were underway.

Source: City of Bath

Zoning

The city adopted a new land use code in September

The City Council adopted a new Land Use Code and zoning map on Sept. 2. The city says the rewrite aligns the rules with the 2023 Comprehensive Plan, simplifies regulations, and is meant to support housing and economic growth. The new code and map are posted on the city's website.

Infrastructure

A sewer and pump station upgrade is planned on Harward Street

The city plans to replace a major sewer main and pump station on Harward Street to reduce overflows that happen when heavy rain overwhelms the treatment plant. The Harward area is the largest part of Bath's sewer system. The project is part of a broader upgrade paid for with a voter-approved bond in 2023.

Source: City of Bath  ·  Illustration for The Maine Ledger

Shipyard

Bath Iron Works is expanding parking along Washington Street

In May, Bath Iron Works presented plans for a 940-space parking garage along Washington Street, a site plan the city approved in March, plus expanded surface lots nearby. The shipyard said parking has been a barrier to hiring. A new supervisor of shipbuilding office building is expected to seek site-plan approval later this year, with construction listed for 2028.

Source: Portland Press Herald  ·  Illustration for The Maine Ledger

Schools

A new elementary school site has not been chosen yet

RSU 1 is planning a new elementary school to replace Dike-Newell School. Minutes from Aug. 24, excerpted by Our School Board, said land behind Bath Middle School is no longer being considered, while the former Dike-Newell site on Wright Drive and one other property remain under study. A referendum is aimed at June 2027.

This week in history

A Bath nurse wrote home from illness in November 1864

On Nov. 14, 1864, Sarah Sampson of Bath wrote that she was still too ill to return to her Civil War relief work in Washington and suggested her sister take her place. After the war, she helped establish the Maine Military and Naval Children's Home in Bath and served as its first director.

Source: Maine Historical Society / Maine Memory Network · Illustration for The Maine Ledger
